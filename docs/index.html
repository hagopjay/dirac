What you are seeing

The event. A proton–proton collision at 13.6 TeV from the Pythia 8.318 generator (hard QCD, partonic pT above 400 GeV, seed 41, event 26 of 30): two back-to-back jets. The detector response is a truth-level toy, not a simulation. Every track is one ribbon in a merged strip with its own birth time, species and momentum, drawn by one shared shader; the needles on the calorimeter crown are the real tower energies.

Time. Light moves 0.9 m per screen second here, so at × 1 one second on screen is 3.0 ns of the collision: a slow-down of 3.3 × 10⁸. The page owns the clock and writes it into the five shaders every frame, so the dial can slow it a further thousand times, stop it, or run it backwards. The sparks at the vertex are a native particle system and only run near demo speed.

Camera. The auto-tour flies a 54-second loop on its own clock: it starts end-on down the beam, pushes in through the barrel, sweeps low past the side, climbs over the top and pulls back out. It keeps moving when the collision is slowed, stopped or reversed, so stopping time gives you bullet time. Drag to take the camera and orbit the vertex, scroll or pinch to zoom, double-click or press T to hand it back to the tour. The view buttons fly to fixed shots; the glow gets stronger as the camera lines up with the beam.

Score. Generated by a hosted music model from a style prompt (cinematic orchestral, pulsing tremolo build into a sub-bass impact). A 24-second loop is cut from it so the impact lands on every second flash. With "Follows time" on, the score is a tape: it slows and drops in pitch with the clock and plays backwards when time does.

Look. Look v3 of the Loom scene builder: v2 folded together with the best of an outside fidelity pass on this page. From that pass: tighter ribbon cores with soft edges whose width follows the render size, machined detector shells (panel seams, ribs, readout traces, key-light glints), a sharper flash with a shock ring, two light shafts in the haze, a multiscale bloom with exposure, a soft highlight shoulder and a vignette, and the optics tools below. Kept from v2: the haze brightness and the vertex sparks. New: a red hot end on the calorimeter crown.

Optics. Glow renders the whole scene into an offscreen target and blooms it. Render quality sets that target's height (its width follows the stage); Adaptive starts at 1080 and drops to 720 on a slow device. Exposure and Bloom act on the glow pass. Save 4K frame renders one 3840 × 2160 still with the event held. The frame-rate readout is measured on your device.

Checks. The scenes are generated by the Loom scene builder, never hand-edited. The Loom hep-event witness checks that every visible particle is drawn exactly once, bends the right way for its charge and momentum, starts at its vertex and stays inside the detector; it passes on these look v3 scenes. Renderer: X_ITE 16.5.1, hosted with this page.
